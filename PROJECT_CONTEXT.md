# Project Context

## Website

Professional B2B corporate website for a physical-products company.

## Business

The company sells physical products to businesses as ingredients/raw materials.

Some products are also sold directly to consumers.

Company-specific details will be provided later.

## Current Objective

Build a polished, professional homepage first.

The homepage should communicate:

- what the company offers
- who it serves
- product categories
- applications/industries
- company credibility
- quality/trust
- a clear path to contact or request a quote

## Reference

https://www.lubrizol.com/

Use this only as inspiration for:

- B2B professionalism
- information architecture
- product presentation
- application storytelling
- visual hierarchy
- credibility
- editorial layouts

Do NOT clone the reference website.

Do NOT copy its branding, text, assets, distinctive layouts, or visual identity.

The final website must have an original visual identity appropriate for this company.

## Design Direction

The website should feel:

- premium
- sophisticated
- established
- confident
- clean
- editorial
- human
- restrained
- trustworthy
- image-led

Avoid:

- generic AI aesthetics
- generic SaaS landing-page styling
- excessive gradients
- glassmorphism
- floating blobs
- excessive rounded cards
- excessive shadows
- meaningless decorative elements
- excessive animations
- repetitive card grids
- generic corporate buzzwords

Prioritize:

- typography
- whitespace
- composition
- photography
- visual hierarchy
- clear navigation
- thoughtful responsive design

## Content Rules

Company-specific information is currently incomplete.

NEVER invent:

- certifications
- statistics
- years in business
- customers
- partnerships
- awards
- production capacity
- manufacturing capabilities
- regulatory approvals
- sustainability claims
- quality claims
- countries served
- product specifications

Use clearly marked placeholders when information is missing.

## Current Scope

Build the homepage only.

Do not introduce:

- authentication
- databases
- unnecessary backend infrastructure
- payments
- CMS
- complex state management

unless a future requirement explicitly requires them.

## Future Information

- industries/applications
- consumer products
- geographic markets
- company capabilities
- quality/certification information
- approved company messaging
- real imagery and assets

Until then, use placeholders rather than assumptions.
